The scene: the inside of an artist's one-room studio flat in Torkkelinmaki,
Kallio, Helsinki, at night in 2003. THE SUBJECT IS THE WORK. Covering most of
the bare wooden floor, seen from above, lies an enormous unfinished map of the
city built out of thousands of small cut paper receipts pasted flat to the
boards - a cardboard city, streets and blocks assembled from till slips and
tram tickets in creams, browns and faded pinks. It nearly lines up and does not
quite. One corner of it is propped up by a fat sealed envelope. Near the edge
of the paper city there are scissors, a paste pot, a craft knife and drifts of
offcuts.

Around the rim of the room, not in the middle: a low mattress with a blanket, a

paraffin heater, stacked canvases leaning face-in against the wall, one tall
sash window with the orange glow of a street lamp and bare branches beyond, a
bare bulb on a flex. A small framed family photograph on the windowsill.
